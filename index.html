Revision is not a DR problem — seven of twelve SET-session closes were rewritten overnight, and one of them erases yesterday's own headline

This page has spent two runs building a doctrine that marginal numbers are provisional on DR feeds. That framing was too narrow, and today's data says so plainly. Monday's completed SET-session closes came back rewritten in seven of twelve names: PTT 38.25 → 38.50, CNTECH01 26.50 → 26.25, CK 18.30 → 18.20, NDX01 24.70 → 24.80, GOLD19 12.40 → 12.50, DELTA 273 → 272, SET 1,621.76 → 1,621.90. These are not thin depositary receipts on foreign underlyings; PTT and DELTA are among the most heavily traded names on the exchange, and PTT's Monday volume was itself revised 68.2m → 75.6m. The correction that matters: yesterday's lead claim on PTT — "the first close beneath the 38.50 zone floor in five sessions, and it closed on the low" — never happened. PTT closed on 38.50, not under it. CNTECH01's "closed exactly ON the 26.50 gate" is likewise gone; it closed 26.25, beneath it. Two of yesterday's stated observations were wrong by the following morning, and neither was about a DR.

The practical upgrade is that the rule gets stronger and simpler rather than weaker. It was never really about which feed — it was that a close is a draft until the next session confirms it, and the smaller the penetration the more likely the draft moves. That is precisely why the four redraws taken today were all large: OIL03 closed 2.33% above its trigger, NDX01 1.81% above with the whole session clear of the level, META01 3.60% through. Against that, the typical overnight revision measured across all seventeen names today was 0.5–0.6% of price. A break that is six times the size of the noise is a break; a break the size of the noise is a rumour. That is now the test, and it is arithmetic rather than instinct. The one case where it was applied against the grain is PTT, and it is flagged below rather than acted on.
