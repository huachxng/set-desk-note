The doctrine now has both halves demonstrated in consecutive runs: yesterday a live macro print was wrong by seven dollars, today ten of twelve live equity prints were wrong in magnitude and five in direction. A number is a draft until the session that produced it is finished — and this is the first run in which that rule was written down before the evidence arrived rather than after.

OIL03 is redrawn a second time in two runs, in the opposite direction — and the real finding is that the redraw rule is mis-specified for a lagging DR

OIL03 closed 3.24, -7.95%, with a session high of 3.24 and a low of 3.14 on 3.08m units. The entire session traded beneath the 3.26 cut-loss and the close is 4.14% beneath the 3.38 support — roughly seven times the one-tick noise on this instrument, which clears the six-times standard this page set for itself, with the whole-session-clear signature on top. Under the rule as written this is unambiguously a break, and the chart has been redrawn: 3.52 / 3.38 / 3.10–3.24 / 3.06 / 2.94. And it may well be wrong by tomorrow morning, for exactly the reason yesterday's was wrong by this morning. Crude is $76.44, +1.74%, as this is written — it closed $75.13 on Tuesday and has traded 74.24–76.47 since. OIL03 spent Wednesday marking Tuesday's -6.15% collapse while the thing it tracks was already bouncing. Par against the live $76.44 implies ≈3.35, some 3.4% above where the DR closed. Yesterday this page redrew OIL03 upward onto a stale crude spike and was wrong in a session. Today it has redrawn it downward onto a stale crude collapse. The direction is opposite; the mechanism is identical.

That is worth more than another apology, because twice in two days is a specification error rather than bad luck. The redraw rule keys off the DR's own close. For an instrument whose price is a one-session-old echo of a 24-hour underlying, the DR's close is never the freshest information available about it — the underlying's settled close is. So the rule is amended for OIL03 and OIL03 only: its ladder moves on a settled WTI close, not on a SET-session DR close. Today's redraw is left standing because the rule in force when the session ended was the old one and this page does not backdate its own rules — but it is flagged as provisional, and if WTI settles Wednesday above $76 the 3.10–3.24 zone is stale on arrival. The position remains retired throughout, on roll decay and tracking failure, which is a structural reason and has now been immune to two consecutive false technical signals in opposite directions. The drawing and the decision are still allowed to disagree, and the decision has still not been wrong.
